# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Handing over
- **Drop boxes: photos from people who do not have Immich.** An owner makes a drop box on the Services tab and shares its link or QR code; guests open it with no account, choose photos and send them, and they arrive in the owner's library tagged `inbox/<name>`. It is write-only: guests see nothing of the library, not even what they sent. An owner sets an end date, the most per file and in all, photos only and a password, can pause, resume or revoke the box, and reads its log. The box has a key of its own with five permissions (`user.read`, `tag.read`, `tag.create`, `tag.asset`, `asset.upload`); files are checked by their content, not their name, and streamed to Immich without being kept. Abuse limits: per guest, 30 requests in ten minutes and 300 files an hour; per box, four uploads at once and an automatic pause (with a notification) after a flood of files or refusals. New setting `DROPBOX_CLIENT_IP_HEADER`. **Guests have no Immich login, so `/drop/` on the page must be reachable without a sign-in in front of it**; nothing is open until someone makes a drop box. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich), [Letting guests reach a drop box](docs/operations.md#letting-guests-reach-a-drop-box) and [Security](docs/security.md#drop-boxes). Adds two tables to the data file. The guest page is in all seven languages; the owner's side of it (the form, the row and the log) is in English in the others until it is translated. (#56, #74)
- **Drop boxes: approval, filing, co-hosts and a clock.** A box can hold every file until a host approves it (kept on the server's disk in the data folder under `dropbox-pending`, not in backups, deleted after two weeks without a decision; the owner sees thumbnails and approves or rejects some or all). A box can file what arrives into an **album** (made when the first photo arrives; the box's key then also gets `album.read`, `album.create` and `albumAsset.create`) and under a **tag of its own**; a photo sent twice is stored once and the guest is told. An owner can add **co-hosts** by email, who manage the box (approve, pause, extend) or only look, and who never see the library or the key; only the owner can end the box or choose co-hosts. A box can **open later and close at an exact time**, sends a reminder about a day before it closes and a summary when it has closed (through `NOTIFY_URL`), shows its files, guests and busiest hour, and can be **extended** by a day or a week. Adds columns and four tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#61, #62, #64, #66)

- **Drop boxes: a friendlier guest page, more ways in, templates, and taking back.** The guest page sends each file on its own with a progress bar, offers the camera on a phone and accepts dropped files, carries on after a lost connection (file by file, with growing pauses) and remembers in the guest's browser what it has sent so a reload does not send it twice; an owner can have photos shrunk to 1600, 2400 or 4000 pixels in the guest's browser (off by default; a shrunk photo loses its metadata), and can set the page's colour, language and cover photo; the page can be installed on a phone under the box's name. A box can have a **short link** (`/d/<code>`) beside its long one, a **printable card** with a QR code, and a **new long link** that ends the old one; each way in is ended on its own (a way in by email is not offered: it needs mailbox access). An owner can save a box's settings as a **template** or **copy** a box, and start boxes from templates (Wedding, Birthday and School trip are built in). A box can let **guests take back what they sent** while it is still in the inbox (a waiting file is deleted; a photo goes to the owner's trash; once the owner has filed it, it is theirs); that box's key also has `asset.read` and `asset.delete`, and it can only be chosen when a box is made. Adds columns and three tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#63, #65, #71, #72)

- **Drop boxes: a shot list, a guest book, and sharing the photos back.** A box can have a **shot list** (up to twenty prompts) that guests choose from for each photo, with a checklist that ticks what already has a photo; a photo for a prompt is also tagged `inbox/<box>/<prompt>`, so Immich lists them grouped, and every photo can be made to name its prompt. A box can have a **guest book**: guests leave a message (up to 1000 characters) and a recorded **voice note** (up to 90 seconds), kept in the app's own data file (so in backups), readable and playable only by the owner and the co-hosts who manage the box, removable and downloadable as a zip. The owner can **share the photos back**: an album with what the box received and a view-only Immich shared link (days, optional password, thank-you message) that guests open with no account to see and download; a closed box's link then leads to it. Immich allows downloads only on a link that shows photo details, so guests with the link see every photo's metadata. Adds columns and four tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#67, #70, #73)

- **Drop boxes: a wall, a best-of, and handing the set on.** A box can have a **wall**: a link, with no sign-in and of its own (renewable and ended on its own), that shows the box's photos full screen as they arrive, as a single photo, a collage or a grid, with captions from the shot list and the guest-book messages the owner chooses; the box's key gets `asset.view` for it, added with the owner's own session and taken off when the wall stops. A **best-of** looks at a box's photos and leaves out the too dark, too bright and blurred ones and all but the sharpest of near-identical shots within an hour (relaxed, normal or strict), puts the rest in an album of the owner's, never deletes or changes anything, and says why each photo was left out; running it again corrects the album. The owner can **hand a box's photos on** (everything, or the best-of) to a service they send to, through the hand-over that exists, so its limits, rules, expiry and take-back apply, and a box can hand everything on by itself once when it closes; the summary and the log say what went where. Adds columns and five tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#60, #68, #69)

## 1.2.0

### Handing over
- **Hand-overs that expire, and taking the last hand-over back.** An owner can make the copies that arrive **expire**: after N days an unfiled copy (in no album, still carrying the inbox tag) goes to the owner's **trash**, never deleted for good; filing it in an album or taking the inbox tag off keeps it. A sender who joined from the page can **Take back the last hand-over** for 24 hours: the receiver's copies go to the receiver's trash, the originals come back from the sender's trash without the service tag, a copy the receiver already filed in an album stays, and a stack or a live photo is taken back whole. Both need the receiver's key to be able to move photos to the trash, so there is a new setting: `RECEIVER_DELETE`, **on by default**. See [Hand-overs that expire](docs/using.md#hand-overs-that-expire), [Taking back the last hand-over](docs/using.md#taking-back-the-last-hand-over) and [Security](docs/security.md#what-a-key-can-do). Adds a column and a table to the data file. (#53, #57)
  - **Warning, read before upgrading: with the default `RECEIVER_DELETE=on`, the receiver keys of the services made by earlier versions are given `asset.delete` (move to the trash) the next time their owner opens the page after the app restarts.** The app cannot do it at boot: only the owner's own login can change a key, so it happens when each owner next opens the page (which is also when they set a lifetime). The app never deletes for good, and it moves to the trash only copies it handed over itself that were never filed in an album. To keep keys as they are, set `RECEIVER_DELETE=off` in `.env` **before** restarting: new keys are then made without the permission, and a key that already has it **loses it** the next time its owner opens the page after a restart with the setting off (the same way round). Keys you made by hand in `.env` or the routes file are never changed by the app; add `asset.delete` to them yourself if you want these two features. `immich-handoff doctor` warns about a key that does not match the setting.
- **Quiet hours, rules about what a service accepts, and photos tagged for a date.** An owner can say a service hands photos over only between two times (`01:00` to `06:00`), optionally only for files above a size, so a slow line is used at night; the administrator can set the same for the whole app (`QUIET_HOURS`, `QUIET_LARGE_MB`, and `TZ` for the time zone). An owner can also say what a service takes in: a minimum rating, photos only, landscape or portrait, a largest file. A photo that does not match is not handed over and gets the refused tag, and the activity says so; this makes a service a feed for a photo frame. A sender can tag a photo for a date (`share/2027-06-01`) and it is handed over on that day. All three are on the owner's service settings, the routes file and, for a plain install, the environment, and are in every language. Adds seven columns to the data file. See [Quiet hours and what a service accepts](docs/using.md#quiet-hours-and-what-a-service-accepts) and [Tagging a photo for a date](docs/using.md#tagging-a-photo-for-a-date). (#55, #50, #52)
- **A stack is handed over whole.** Tagging any photo of a stack (a burst, or a RAW and JPEG pair) hands over every photo in it, the cover first, and makes the stack again at the receiver's, with the same cover. With **move** the whole stack goes to the sender's trash; each photo counts against a daily limit. This needs `stack.read` on the sender's key and `stack.create` on the receiver's: new keys have them, and a key made earlier is given them when its owner next opens the page. Until then, or for a key made by hand without them, only the tagged photo goes (or the photos arrive separately) and the setup check warns. (#25)
- **A live photo is handed over with its motion.** The motion clip (a separate hidden video in Immich) goes too, hidden, and the still is linked to it, so the receiver's copy plays. With **move**, the still and its clip go to the sender's trash together. If the clip cannot be read only the still is handed over, as before. (#24)
- **Details that Immich keeps only in its database now arrive.** An **archived** photo stays archived at the receiver, and the **description, rating and location** a person added in Immich travel as an XMP sidecar the receiver's Immich reads (the photo file and the checksum check are unchanged). Edits (rotate, crop) still do not travel. On by default; `CARRY_DETAILS=off` turns it off for the whole app. It needs no new permission. A description arrives as the receiver's own, so a sender who keeps private notes there should know. See [what is carried over](docs/what-is-carried-over.md) and [settings](docs/settings.md). (#23)
- **Hand over a whole album.** A sender can link one of their own albums to a service they joined from the page. Everything in it, now and added later, is handed over once, with no tag; the key gets `album.read` through the person's own login. Albums are checked on a timer (`ALBUM_CHECK_MINUTES`, default 15) because Immich has no album event. A big album hand-over adds a line to Recent events and notifies `NOTIFY_URL`. Adds two columns to the data file.
- **Arrivals can go into an album.** A service can name an album (**More options** when creating it on the page, `album:` in a routes file, `ALBUM` for a plain install). Handed-over photos go into that album of the receiver's account, made when the first photo arrives, instead of being tagged inbox; if it cannot be used the photo is tagged inbox as before and the problem is reported once. Such a key also gets `album.read`, `album.create` and `albumAsset.create`. Adds a column to the data file, so going back to an older version afterwards is not supported.
- **Pause and resume.** A sender can pause their own sending to a service, and an owner a whole service: nothing is handed over, photos tagged meanwhile stay tagged, and on resume they go at once. Each side sees the other's pause. See [Using it](docs/using.md#pausing). (#7)
- **Daily limits per sender.** An owner can cap what each sender hands over in a day (photos, megabytes, or both). Photos over the cap wait, still tagged, and go by themselves when the oldest hand-over is 24 hours old or the cap is raised. Senders see their use and the wait, owners see each sender's use, and `NOTIFY_URL` is told once. Adds columns and a small table to the data file. See [Using it](docs/using.md#daily-limits-for-senders). (#12)

### The page
- **A new look.** The page is redesigned: the twilight palette of the app icon in a light and a dark theme (a button in the bar switches; the choice is remembered in the browser), glass-style cards, fonts the app serves itself, and three views chosen from tabs (**Overview**, **Services**, **Activity**). The Overview opens with a **Needs you** strip (requests, lost or removed connections, paused services, reached limits) and **This week**, a chart of the last seven days with what waits and what is in your inbox; new people see a **Get started** guide. Services are rows that open in place, grouped into **You send to**, **You receive**, **You offer** and **You can join**, and every question is asked in a dialog in the page's style. The bar has **Search** (a command palette on **Ctrl K** / **Cmd K**) and an arrival chime. The light behind the page drifts, cards tilt toward a mouse and numbers roll, all skipped for reduced motion and touch screens, and nothing runs while idle. The sign-in, expired-invitation and invitation pages, the offer form and the administrator's page follow the same look. Nothing a service can do changed. (#3)
- **A live scene on the Overview.** Each service you take part in has a stage with a lane for every sender and a gate into the receiving library. For every real hand-over a small square travels along the lane and an **arrived while you watch** counter ticks; a paused lane, a daily limit (with when it ends) and a lost connection are shown with words as well as colour. It shows no photo or file name, does nothing while idle and respects reduced motion. It is built on a live stream of typed events (a photo started, handed over, refused or failed, held by a limit, a pause, a lost or restored connection), each sent only to the people it concerns and to an Immich administrator, a burst being one event with a count. See [Using it](docs/using.md#the-live-scene) and [How it works inside](docs/architecture.md#live-events).
- **The pages in seven languages.** English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese (Brazilian wording) and Russian. The page follows your browser's language, and the **language menu** in the bar (the two-letter code) picks one for good (a cookie in this browser). Only what you read on the pages is translated: the log, the setup check, the documentation and the `NOTIFY_URL` messages stay English. Events already in the activity list are shown in your language too. The translations are first drafts and a native speaker's corrections are welcome (see [How it works inside](docs/architecture.md#languages)). (#16)
- **Install the page on a phone.** The page has a web app manifest, icons in the sizes phones ask for and theme colours, so it can be added to the home screen: **Install app** in Chrome on Android, **Share, Add to Home Screen** in Safari on iPhone. It needs HTTPS. There is no service worker and nothing is cached, so no signed-in page is ever kept. See [Operations](docs/operations.md#installing-the-page-on-a-phone).
- **A way into the page from inside Immich.** The app serves `static/immich-button.js`; one line added by your reverse proxy to Immich's pages loads it, and it puts the Handoff icon in Immich's own header, between the notification bell and the person's avatar, so nobody has to type the page's address. It falls back to a round button at the bottom right if Immich's header changes. [Make Handoff easy to find](docs/find-handoff.md) has the tested nginx configuration, the login-page message tip and what does not work. A browser test checks the placement against the pinned Immich. (#44)
- **Share the page and invitations more easily.** Next to an invitation link are **Copy a message** (a short ready-written invitation in the page's language) and **QR code** (drawn by the app, black on white so a phone can scan it from the screen); the administrator's page has a **Share this page** box with the same. Adds one dependency, `rsc.io/qr`. (#44)
- **The page notices when Tags are off** in a person's Immich account (Immich has them off by default) and offers a **Turn on Tags** button, which changes that one setting with their own session. They still need to refresh Immich afterwards.

### Running it
- **A setup check.** `docker compose exec immich-handoff /immich-handoff doctor` tests the setup and says how to fix what fails: the secrets, `PUBLIC_WEBHOOK_URL`, that Immich is reachable and new enough, that each stored key works and has the permissions it needs, that Tags are on, that each sender's workflow exists and matches, how Immich's last workflow runs ended, and the backup settings. It only reads, and exits 1 when something failed (warnings do not). Keys made from now on also get the read-only `workflow.logs`; older keys work and the check says how to add it. See [Troubleshooting](docs/troubleshooting.md#check-your-setup-first). (#13)
- **An administrator's page.** An Immich administrator gets an **Admin** link (everyone else gets "not found") listing every service with its owner, members and the health of each connection, recent events across all services, **Check now** (the setup check on demand), and the power to **remove a service or a member**. Removal makes the app forget the key and stop handing over, tells the people involved and `NOTIFY_URL`; it cannot delete anyone's API key (only its owner can), so the person is told to delete it (**Clean up** does). See [The administrator's page](docs/admin.md). (#14)
- **Backup and restore.** `immich-handoff backup` asks the running app for a backup of its data file; the app makes it at the next quiet moment between hand-overs (after 4 hours it is made anyway, named `-forced`, with a warning). `backup --wait` follows it with an exit status a script can read (0 made, 2 made but forced, 1 failed), `backup --list` shows the files, and with the app stopped it copies at once. `immich-handoff restore <file>` checks the file, refuses while the app runs and refuses to overwrite data without `--force`. The app now holds a lock on its data folder, so a second copy on the same data stops with a clear message. See [Operations](docs/operations.md#backups), which also has a walkthrough for moving to another machine. (#15)
- **The app makes backups on its own.** By default every 24 hours (`BACKUP_EVERY`, or `off`), waiting for a quiet moment, keeping the newest 7 (`BACKUP_KEEP`); only these `handoff-auto-…` files are ever removed. `BACKUP_DIR` can point at a second mounted folder. A failure is logged, written to the recent events, sent to `NOTIFY_URL` and tried again an hour later; the administrator's page shows the schedule, the newest backup and the last failure. See [Automatic backups](docs/operations.md#automatic-backups). (#29)
- **Download a backup from the administrator's page, if you switch it on.** `HANDOFF_ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD=true` adds a **Download backup** button. It is off by default because the file holds every stored key (encrypted) and everyone's name and email. When on: administrators only, a confirmation, one download every 10 minutes for the whole app, each one written to the recent events and sent to `NOTIFY_URL`, the copy removed as soon as it is sent. See [Download a backup](docs/operations.md#download-a-backup-from-the-administrators-page) and [Security](docs/security.md#backups). (#28)
- **Nightly images.** Every night at 09:00 UTC, and only if `main` changed, an image is built from `main`, tested like a release, signed, and published to Docker Hub and GitHub's registry as `20261107-nightly` (the date) and `nightly` (always the newest), so `image: vfuoglio/immich-handoff:nightly` follows `main`. The newest 14 dated tags are kept; `latest` and release tags are never touched. See [Releasing](docs/releasing.md#nightly-images) and [Operations](docs/operations.md#upgrading). The workflow has not run on GitHub yet.
- **Release hygiene.** Every release image is **signed** (cosign, keyless) and carries a **software bill of materials** and build provenance, and the workflow checks both. The GitHub Actions are current and **pinned by commit hash**; Dependabot proposes weekly updates into `develop`. A fix for an older version (1.1.1 after 1.2.0) no longer takes the `latest` tag. (#9)
- **Container health check and Docker Hub.** The image reports `healthy` or `unhealthy` in `docker ps` (a built-in `immich-handoff --healthcheck`; the image has no shell or curl), and is also published on Docker Hub as `vfuoglio/immich-handoff` besides `ghcr.io/vfuoglio/immich-handoff`.

### Tests and documentation
- **The end-to-end tests run in three groups at the same time.** They used to run one after another, about five minutes, and CI waited for the unit tests first. Now `test/groups.sh` splits them into three groups of about the same length (`browser`, `core`, `hub`), CI runs the three together on separate machines (each with its own throwaway Immich) and does not wait for the unit tests, so a pull request waits for the longest group, not for the sum. The check that branch protection asks for, `e2e`, is kept, green only when every group is; a script fails when a test is in no group or in two. `test/run.sh browser` (or `core`, `hub`) runs one group, and `test/run-parallel.sh` runs the three on one machine (only faster with CPU to spare). A race in the carry-over test (a hand-over could carry the date from before an edit that Immich had not applied yet) showed up under the load and is fixed. (#58)
- **The page is tested in a real browser.** The end-to-end run also opens the real page in headless Chrome, including a phone-sized window, and checks what a person sees and does (see [How it works inside](docs/architecture.md#testing)). It found and fixed three faults: the numbers on the Overview refreshed in a loop for as long as the page was open, the rolling digits never rolled after the first paint, and an open service row closed itself after every action. A test also checks what is carried over against a real Immich (`test/e2e/carryover_test.go`).
- **The pictures in the README and the documentation are remade** for the new design (all in English), and on a phone the bar's buttons no longer spill off the right edge. The README moves: an animation of the live scene near the top and a **In action** table of six more (the numbers rolling during a hand-over on a desktop and a phone, the command palette, the theme switch, a service opening in place, an invitation with its QR code), all recorded from the real page in the light and the dark theme; [how they are made](tools/screenshots/README.md#the-animations).

### Fixes
- **A large video no longer fails because moving it took more than two minutes.** A transfer now has no total limit and is given up only after three minutes without any data moving.
- An install with only fixed accounts (no data file) showed an empty heading and no **Needs you** line or numbers on the Overview.

## 1.1.0
- **Prebuilt images:** each release publishes a multi-architecture image (amd64, arm64) to `ghcr.io/vfuoglio/immich-handoff` and a GitHub Release. The release workflow runs the full test suite first and smoke-tests the image on both architectures. `immich-handoff --version` prints the version. See [Releasing](docs/releasing.md).
- **Tags must be switched on in Immich by every person who takes part, and Immich must be refreshed afterwards.** The documentation now says so everywhere it matters.
- **Photos filed in an album lose their inbox tag within minutes**, not up to a day later. Immich sends no event for albums, so a small timer does the check (`CLEANUP_INTERVAL_MINUTES`, default 15; `0` = daily only). The docs explain the limitation.
- Fix: the invitation page lost its styling and buttons behind a path prefix, and accepting an invitation ended on an error page.
- **The sender decides what happens to their originals**, when they join: keep them (the default) or move them to their trash. The owner of a service no longer sets this. The key made for a sender who keeps their originals has no delete permission. Existing members keep the behaviour they had.
- Fixed-account services (`.env`, routes file) now **default to copy** (`HANDOFF_MODE=copy`). Set `HANDOFF_MODE=move`, or `mode: move` on a service, to keep the old behaviour.
- Services created on the page get their own nested inbox tag (`inbox/<name>`) by default, so one owner with several services sees the arrivals separately.
- The page: the offer form is a collapsed section, the last run is described in plain words, a pending request no longer leaves an empty row, cards are tidier.
- Fix: a photo handed over a moment ago is no longer handed over again while Immich's search still lists it.
- `WEBHOOK_SECRET` must be at least 16 characters.
- `.env.example` is now hub-first: a fresh install needs no API keys.
- Documentation reorganised into `docs/` with screenshots; tooling to remake them in `tools/screenshots`.
- The release workflow can be started by hand from the Actions tab.
