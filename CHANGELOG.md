# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Look and feel
- **Two colour schemes, each in light and dark.** The pages come in **Twilight** (the indigo, magenta and orange of the app's icon, unchanged and still the default) and **Immich** (calm blues on near-neutral greys). Everyone chooses from the new palette button in the bar, next to the light/dark button, and the choice is remembered in their own browser. The administrator sets where people start with `THEME` (`twilight` or `immich`) and, if wanted, `THEME_MODE` (`auto`, `light` or `dark`); see [Settings](docs/settings.md). A phone's address bar takes the colour of the scheme. A drop box's own accent colour now also wins over both schemes.
- **A new look, made for the phone as much as for the desktop.** A pill in the middle of the bar slides a gradient under the view you are in (Overview, Services, Activity); on a phone it becomes a tab bar at the bottom of the screen. Cards are glass with a soft light that follows the pointer, buttons are pills, checkboxes are switches, dialogs rise from the bottom on a phone, every view fades in, and a search bar under each headline opens the command palette.
- **The Overview, re-ordered.** The date, the greeting, the live stream and the search bar come first; then **Needs you** as rows (what it says on the left, its buttons on the right, **A** and **D** answering a request from the keyboard), the week's numbers and the guide. **G** then **O**, **S** or **A** switches view from the keyboard.
- **The wall and the frame, animated.** One engine now runs both: choose how a photo gives way to the next (**Fade**, **Zoom**, **Slide**, **Rise**) and what is behind it (a faint **Glow** in your colour scheme, or **Black**) with buttons that appear when you move the pointer, or in the address (`?fx=zoom&bg=black`); the screen remembers it. A photo that arrives is shown at once, stays a little longer and gets a soft ring; in the grid and the collage the new one springs in and the rest glide aside; a host's message rises in and fades out; with no photo yet a ring breathes slowly. Nothing flashes, a screen with little processor or memory drops the zoom and the drifting glow by itself, and reduced motion leaves a short dissolve. A frame now has the same buttons. (#109)
- **The live scene is a stream, in both directions.** Photos handed to you fly into **You** as glowing squares with a trail and a ring where they land; photos **you** send fly away from **You** as outlined squares with an arrow, on lanes whose dashes crawl the way the photos go. It is still driven only by what really happens.

### Notifications
- **Notifications through ntfy, Home Assistant or any JSON endpoint, several at once.** One sender now carries every message: it queues them and sends in the background (a slow server no longer holds up a hand-over), tries again when the other side's server fails, logs a refusal or a redirect instead of ignoring it, and never writes the address to the log. `NOTIFY_URL` still works and now sends a title, a priority, a tag and, with the new `PUBLIC_APP_URL`, a link to the page. The new `NOTIFY_FILE` lists more destinations, ntfy or `json`, each taking only the kinds it asks for (`problem`, `backup`, `bridge`, `dropbox`, `info`). The messages about a stored key that stopped working and about drop boxes, which the page and the documentation promised, now go out the same way as all the others, and the message about a pass that went wrong is written in words. See [Notifications](docs/operations.md#notifications). (#96)
- **Notifications by email.** A destination with `format: email` in `NOTIFY_FILE` sends the messages as mail through your SMTP server (STARTTLS by default, implicit TLS or, for a trusted relay, none; never a password without protection). Messages that arrive within a couple of minutes (`group`) become one mail with a line each; each mail has a plain-text and a simple HTML part and a link to the page. See [Notifications](docs/operations.md#notifications). (#97)
- **Email to the people taking part.** A person can turn on **Email me** on the Overview and get an email about what happens to their services: the same lines as their Activity list, in the language of their page, gathered for a few minutes into one email, at most six an hour, either everything or only what needs them. A test email, and a link in every email (also the one-click header mail programs use) stops it without signing in. It is off for everyone until they turn it on, and needs a destination with `people: true` in `NOTIFY_FILE`, `PUBLIC_APP_URL` and `HANDOFF_STORE_KEY`. Adds one table to the data file. See [Email to the people taking part](docs/operations.md#notifications). (#100)
- **Notifications on people's own devices (web push).** A person can turn on **Notifications on this device** on the Overview and get a notification on that phone or computer with the page closed: the lines of their Activity list, plus "N photos arrived from Bob in Holiday" for receivers and "N photos were not handed over" for a sender whose photos the receiver has in the trash. Each device has its own choice (everything, or only what needs the person), its own language, a test button and a remove button; what happens within about ten seconds is one notification. The app only calls the push services of Google, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft, and encrypts every message for the browser (RFC 8291) with no library beyond Go's own. On when people can join from the page and there is a contact for the push services (`PUBLIC_APP_URL` over https, or `PUSH_CONTACT`); `PUSH=off` switches it off. The page now has a service worker (`static/sw.js`) that only shows notifications: no fetch handler, no cache. Adds one table to the data file. Not tried on a real iPhone. See [Notifications on devices](docs/operations.md#notifications-on-devices). (#99, #5)
- **Test your notifications.** The administrator's page lists the notification destinations (name, format, kinds; never an address) and has a **Send a test notification** button that sends one message to each, whatever kinds it takes, and shows how each answered. `immich-handoff doctor` now lists them and checks the settings, and `doctor --send-test` sends the test from the command line. (#102)
- **Fewer repeats for the administrator.** A hand-over service that keeps failing, or a backup that fails every hour, is now told once, with a reminder after a day and a message when it works again, instead of on every pass. Refused photos alone no longer send a message to the administrator (the receiver's own choice; the page and the sender's notifications show it). The tracker is kept in memory, so a problem that still lasts after a restart is told once more. (#103)
- **Answer from a message.** A mail or notification to a service's owner when someone asks to send now has **Approve** and **Decline** buttons, and when you pause a service or your own sending it has **Resume**. A link shows what it will do and asks first (a mail scanner opening it does nothing), works once for 24 hours, needs no Immich sign-in, and does exactly what the page's button does. Only the person who can do the action gets the link; the two answers to a request cancel each other. Needs `PUBLIC_APP_URL`. Adds one table to the data file. See [Action links](docs/security.md#action-links). (#104)

### Events
- **Drop boxes know how long to keep what guests leave.** A new setting, **Keep what guests leave for (days after it ends)**, chosen when a box is made (30 by default, 0 for until it is revoked) and changed later by the owner under **The event: what is kept afterwards**, says how long the box's guest data (names, hearts, the gallery; see the entries below as they arrive) outlives the box. Templates keep it. Adds a column to the data file. See [An event](docs/using.md#an-event-from-the-first-guest-to-the-last-photo). (#115)
- **A gallery for the guests of an event.** A drop box can now show guests the photos it has kept: switch on **Guests may look at the photos and heart them** (when making the box, or later under **The event: what is kept afterwards**) and the guest page has **The photos so far**, a grid, newest first, that fills in by itself, with a heart on each photo (one a guest, with the count for everybody) and a large view. **and download them** adds a **Download** that saves the original; **and see who sent each photo** makes the page ask for a name (remembered in the guest's browser, cleaned, at most 40 letters) and show it under the photo, and keeps no name at all when it is off. Only photos in the owner's library under the box are shown, never one waiting for approval. After the box ends its link still shows the gallery and the owner's thanks, without a form, until the retention is over. The box's key gets `asset.view` (and `asset.download` if downloads are allowed) through the owner's own sign-in, and loses them when the gallery is switched off; a password on **The whole page** covers the gallery. Adds a table and a few columns to the data file. See [An event](docs/using.md#an-event-from-the-first-guest-to-the-last-photo) and [Security](docs/security.md#drop-boxes). (#116)
- **A moderator link, and a message when files wait.** The owner of a box can make a link, with a QR code, for a helper who has no account: a page that shows the box's name and the files waiting for approval as thumbnails, where they approve or reject some or all of them. It works without a script, shows nothing of the owner's library, link, settings or key, cannot pause or end the box, and is renewed or ended by the owner alone (and with the box). The log says "A moderator approved 3 files". Also, when files start to wait in a box that approves them, the owner is now told once through the notification settings (kind `dropbox`), and again when files wait after the queue was emptied. Adds a column to the data file. See [Moderator link](docs/using.md#moderator-link) and [Security](docs/security.md#drop-boxes). (#117)
- **The wall shows who sent a photo.** In a box that shows names, a photo on the wall is captioned with its sender ("The cake · by Ann", or "by Ann" with no prompt), in the language of the screen. Nothing changes for a box that does not show names. The wall already showed only photos the box had kept, so what waits for approval never appears on it. (#118)
- **Closing an event: retention, a ZIP and a summary with hearts.** What guests left with a box (the names given with photos and every heart) is now deleted when the box's retention ends (once, and at once when the box is revoked), and the log says so; the photos in the owner's library are never touched. The owner can **Download every photo as a ZIP**: the originals of everything the box kept, streamed with the owner's own sign-in (the box's key gets no new permission, nothing is kept on the server, one ZIP of a box at a time, and a request from another site is refused). The closing summary now counts the hearts given. Putting what arrives into an album named after the event was already there (**Album**). (#119)
- **The guest link, after the event, is the album.** After a box has ended its link keeps the gallery, with the owner's thanks, for as long as the box's retention says (the album's expiry), with hearts and, where the owner allows downloads, a new **Download all as a ZIP** for guests (one at a time for a guest, three at a time for a box). A password on the box is the album's PIN. Immich's own shared page (**Share the photos back**) is still there and independent. (#120)
- **Best of an event: hearts, pins and hides.** The owner can **Make the album of loved photos**: an album called "*box* loved by guests" with the photos guests gave hearts and the ones a moderator pinned (pinned first, then the most hearts, at most a hundred, never a hidden one), corrected when run again. On the moderator page, **The photos kept** lists the photos the box kept with their hearts and sender, and each can be **pinned** (always in a best-of) or **hidden** (gone from the guests' gallery, the wall, the guests' ZIP and hearts, and left out of a best-of as "hidden by the host"; it stays in the library and the owner's ZIP). Adds two columns to the data file. See [A best-of](docs/using.md#a-best-of). (#121)
- **An event in the host's own look.** A box can have a **Title guests see** (the box's name stays the internal one, and what the tag and the album are made from) and a **Logo** (JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 300 KB), both set under **The look of the guest page**: the guest page, the album after the event, the wall (the logo in a corner) and the installed app carry them. Whatever colour a host picks is now made to read: text on it is black or white, whichever reaches at least 4.5 to 1 (it could fall short for mid-tone colours before), and text in the colour (links, headings) is made just dark or light enough to reach 4.5 to 1 on a light or dark page. There was and is no app credit on a guest page to hide. Adds a column and a table to the data file. (#122)

### Phone first
- **Send photos from a phone, in pieces that survive a lost connection.** A new **Send** tab (also in the command palette) takes photos and videos from the device's gallery or files and sends them, as they are, to your own Immich with your own sign-in, tagged for the service you pick (the one chosen last is on top) or left in your library only; from the tag on, every hand-over rule applies as for any tagged photo, and the hand-over starts a few seconds after the last photo arrives. Files travel in pieces of at most 4 MB, kept in the data folder (`phone-uploads`, not in backups) until the file is whole, so a dropped connection costs a piece; the page waits for the network and carries on from where the server stands. Checked by what the file really is, by an optional SHA-1, and bounded: 2 GB a file (`PHONE_UPLOAD_FILE_MB`), 4 GB waiting for each person (`PHONE_UPLOAD_WAITING_MB`), unfinished pieces deleted after 48 hours (`PHONE_UPLOAD_KEEP_HOURS`). Needs the data folder. See [Send photos from a phone](docs/using.md#send-photos-from-a-phone). (#123, #124)
- **A queue that carries on by itself.** What a person sends from a phone waits in a list kept in the browser's own database (IndexedDB), file by file with its progress, so a closed page, a restarted phone or a night offline costs nothing: the page says **Waiting for the network**, asks the server where each file stands and goes on from there. **Send again**, **Remove** and **Clear the finished ones**; an **Only on Wi-Fi** switch (a hint: it goes by what the browser says, and Safari says nothing); a question before a video of about 100 MB goes over mobile data; a one-time reminder that locations go with the photos; a message, naming the files, when the browser has no room to keep them. A file is only ever sent as the person who chose it (the app refuses one named for another person). (#125)
- **Share from the gallery, on Android.** The web app manifest now has a `share_target`: once Handoff is installed, it is in the phone's Share menu for photos and videos. The service worker receives the share (its one fetch handler, for `static/share`; it still keeps no cache), keeps the files in the browser's database and opens the Send page, where the person picks the service; nothing is sent before they choose. The Send page registers the worker and, where Chrome offers it, explains and offers **Install Handoff**. Without the worker the share falls back to the page. iPhone has no Share entry for web apps; the Send page's picker is there. (#126)
- **Sending goes on with the screen off, and you are told when it is over.** Where the browser has Background Sync (Android Chrome) the service worker finishes the queue after the page is closed or the screen is off (the page and the worker never send at the same time: a Web Lock gives the line to one of them), and carries on when the network returns; where it has not (iPhone), files go only while the page is open and the page says so. **Tell me when it is done** (everything arrived or something went wrong, only trouble, or never) makes one notice on the device when the queue has emptied, however many photos it held, in the person's language; nothing is said while the person is looking at the page. Where each file stands is now kept as it is sent, so a phone that dies in the middle of a file carries on from the server's piece. A photo sent to a service appears on the Overview's live stream like any hand-over. (#127)

### Sharing and showing
- **A weekly digest of a service.** An owner can turn on a weekly digest: every Monday morning the best photos that arrived in the service during the week (judged by the same rules as a drop box's best-of, at most forty) are put in a new album of theirs and a shared link to it that ends after a month is made; the card shows the link, the counts and what was left out and why, and the owner is told. It uses the service's own key, which gets a few more permissions when it is turned on and loses them when it is turned off. Nothing is deleted or changed. Adds a table to the data file. See [Weekly digest](docs/using.md#weekly-digest). (#79)
- **Frames: an album on a screen with no account.** Anyone who can join can make a frame of one of their own albums: a long link, with a QR code, that shows the album's photos full screen on a photo frame, an old tablet or a TV, a random round at a time, newest first, and picks up photos added to the album (and drops those taken out) within a couple of minutes (`FRAME_REFRESH`). The link can end by itself after 30 days, 90 days or a year, or when its owner ends it, which also deletes the frame's own key (it can only look at pictures and list an album's photos). The page holds no session and shows nothing but the album's photos. Adds a table to the data file. See [Frames](docs/using.md#frames-an-album-on-a-screen) and [Security](docs/security.md#frames). (#78)

### Handing over
- **Photos by email for drop boxes.** If the person who runs the app gives it a mailbox to read (`MAIL_IN_*`, over IMAP, off by default), an owner can give a drop box an address such as `drop+k3m9x2tqaw@example.org`. Photos and videos attached to a mail sent to it arrive like uploads, under the box's limits, approval, filing and log; the text of the mail and everything else is dropped, nobody gets a reply, and the sender's address is never kept (only the same six-character mark a guest has). A box with a password cannot have an address. `doctor` checks the login. The app speaks IMAP with the standard library only. Adds a column to the data file. See [Photos by email](docs/operations.md#photos-by-email) and [Security](docs/security.md#photos-by-email). (#76)
- **Event pools: everybody gets everybody's photos.** An owner can make a service an event pool, with an optional end: everyone who joins, the owner too, both sends and receives, a photo any member tags goes to everyone else (never back to its sender), each copy is tagged `from/<name>` so you can browse by photographer, and nobody's original is touched. Joining asks plainly and needs a yes (kept in the app, not only in the page). The owner can end it now, give it an end, or reopen it; a person who joins late gets what is shared from then on and one who leaves keeps what they received. Adds two columns to the data file. See [Event pool](docs/using.md#event-pool-everybody-gets-everybodys-photos) and [Security](docs/security.md#event-pools). (#51)
- **Bridges: taking a hand-over back, if the receiver allows it.** A receiver on another Immich server may add an optional fifth permission, `asset.delete`, to their key. The sender's **Take back the last hand-over** then also moves the copies that hand-over left on that server to the receiver's trash (never deleted for good, within the same 24 hours), using the ids the app stored, so the key still cannot read anything. The app cannot tell whether the receiver filed a copy in an album, so that copy goes to the trash too (the invitation page says so); without the permission, or after the receiver removes it from the key (checked at every pass and before a take-back), the message says how many copies stayed there. The service's card and `immich-handoff doctor` say which applies. **Letting copies expire still does not work across servers**, on purpose. Adds a column to the data file. The receiver's page is in all seven languages. (#89)
- **Drop boxes: photos from people who do not have Immich.** An owner makes a drop box on the Services tab and shares its link or QR code; guests open it with no account, choose photos and send them, and they arrive in the owner's library tagged `inbox/<name>`. It is write-only: guests see nothing of the library, not even what they sent. An owner sets an end date, the most per file and in all, photos only and a password, can pause, resume or revoke the box, and reads its log. The box has a key of its own with five permissions (`user.read`, `tag.read`, `tag.create`, `tag.asset`, `asset.upload`); files are checked by their content, not their name, and streamed to Immich without being kept. Abuse limits: per guest, 30 requests in ten minutes and 300 files an hour; per box, four uploads at once and an automatic pause (with a notification) after a flood of files or refusals. New setting `DROPBOX_CLIENT_IP_HEADER`. **Guests have no Immich login, so `/drop/` on the page must be reachable without a sign-in in front of it**; nothing is open until someone makes a drop box. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich), [Letting guests reach a drop box](docs/operations.md#letting-guests-reach-a-drop-box) and [Security](docs/security.md#drop-boxes). Adds two tables to the data file. The guest page is in all seven languages; the owner's side of it (the form, the row and the log) is in English in the others until it is translated. (#56, #74)
- **Drop boxes: approval, filing, co-hosts and a clock.** A box can hold every file until a host approves it (kept on the server's disk in the data folder under `dropbox-pending`, not in backups, deleted after two weeks without a decision; the owner sees thumbnails and approves or rejects some or all). A box can file what arrives into an **album** (made when the first photo arrives; the box's key then also gets `album.read`, `album.create` and `albumAsset.create`) and under a **tag of its own**; a photo sent twice is stored once and the guest is told. An owner can add **co-hosts** by email, who manage the box (approve, pause, extend) or only look, and who never see the library or the key; only the owner can end the box or choose co-hosts. A box can **open later and close at an exact time**, sends a reminder about a day before it closes and a summary when it has closed (through `NOTIFY_URL`), shows its files, guests and busiest hour, and can be **extended** by a day or a week. Adds columns and four tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#61, #62, #64, #66)

- **Drop boxes: a friendlier guest page, more ways in, templates, and taking back.** The guest page sends each file on its own with a progress bar, offers the camera on a phone and accepts dropped files, carries on after a lost connection (file by file, with growing pauses) and remembers in the guest's browser what it has sent so a reload does not send it twice; an owner can have photos shrunk to 1600, 2400 or 4000 pixels in the guest's browser (off by default; a shrunk photo loses its metadata), and can set the page's colour, language and cover photo; the page can be installed on a phone under the box's name. A box can have a **short link** (`/d/<code>`) beside its long one, a **printable card** with a QR code, and a **new long link** that ends the old one; each way in is ended on its own (a way in by email is not offered: it needs mailbox access). An owner can save a box's settings as a **template** or **copy** a box, and start boxes from templates (Wedding, Birthday and School trip are built in). A box can let **guests take back what they sent** while it is still in the inbox (a waiting file is deleted; a photo goes to the owner's trash; once the owner has filed it, it is theirs); that box's key also has `asset.read` and `asset.delete`, and it can only be chosen when a box is made. Adds columns and three tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#63, #65, #71, #72)
- **Drop boxes: choosing photos is now a pleasure.** The guest page has a large drop area that wakes up while files are dragged over the page, a tile with a small picture for every chosen file, a **×** on each to take it out before sending, **Remove all**, the number of files and their size, and a **Send** button that always answers (it asks for photos when none are chosen, counts "Sending 2 of 5", and ends with a message in place, with **Try again** for what failed). Everything animates, and nothing does under reduced motion. The page's security policy now allows `blob:` images and audio, which is how a browser shows its own previews.
- **Drop boxes: "Message to the host", and one menu for the password.** What was called the guest book is now **Message to the host** (the guest's name is required, so you know who wrote). The password is chosen from one menu, **Secure with a password...**: **No**, **Upload** (what the password did until now: the page can be seen, sending needs it) or the new **The whole page** (the link shows only a request for the password, nothing of the box, until it is given; a cover, colour, a guest's uploads, messages and sending all stay closed; the right password is remembered in that browser for a week). Boxes that had a password keep guarding the upload.

- **Drop boxes: a shot list, a guest book, and sharing the photos back.** A box can have a **shot list** (up to twenty prompts) that guests choose from for each photo, with a checklist that ticks what already has a photo; a photo for a prompt is also tagged `inbox/<box>/<prompt>`, so Immich lists them grouped, and every photo can be made to name its prompt. A box can have a **guest book**: guests leave a message (up to 1000 characters) and a recorded **voice note** (up to 90 seconds), kept in the app's own data file (so in backups), readable and playable only by the owner and the co-hosts who manage the box, removable and downloadable as a zip. The owner can **share the photos back**: an album with what the box received and a view-only Immich shared link (days, optional password, thank-you message) that guests open with no account to see and download; a closed box's link then leads to it. Immich allows downloads only on a link that shows photo details, so guests with the link see every photo's metadata. Adds columns and four tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#67, #70, #73)

- **Drop boxes: a wall, a best-of, and handing the set on.** A box can have a **wall**: a link, with no sign-in and of its own (renewable and ended on its own), that shows the box's photos full screen as they arrive, as a single photo, a collage or a grid, with captions from the shot list and the guest-book messages the owner chooses; the box's key gets `asset.view` for it, added with the owner's own session and taken off when the wall stops. A **best-of** looks at a box's photos and leaves out the too dark, too bright and blurred ones and all but the sharpest of near-identical shots within an hour (relaxed, normal or strict), puts the rest in an album of the owner's, never deletes or changes anything, and says why each photo was left out; running it again corrects the album. The owner can **hand a box's photos on** (everything, or the best-of) to a service they send to, through the hand-over that exists, so its limits, rules, expiry and take-back apply, and a box can hand everything on by itself once when it closes; the summary and the log say what went where. Adds columns and five tables to the data file. See [Drop boxes](docs/using.md#drop-boxes-photos-from-people-who-do-not-have-immich). (#60, #68, #69)

- **Bridges: hand photos over to someone on another Immich server.** An owner presses **Invite someone on another server** on a service and sends the link; the person opens it (**no login on your server**), makes a key in their own Immich with exactly four permissions (`user.read`, `tag.create`, `tag.asset`, `asset.upload`) and enters their address, the key and a name. From then on every photo tagged for the service goes to the owner **and** to them, tagged `from/<sender>`, and the original is touched only after both hold the copy. The key can upload and tag and nothing else (it cannot read, search, download or delete), so **taking back and expiry do not work on the other server**, and a copy there is trusted on Immich's answer to the upload. The app connects only over `https://` to public addresses, checked again on the real connection, never follows a redirect and never calls `/users` on the other server; a key that can do more than the four things is refused. If the other server cannot be reached, photos wait tagged (**for the owner's own receiver too**), the owner is told once and the app retries with growing pauses; a revoked key stops everything until a new key and link. The card shows the state and the version, `immich-handoff doctor` checks the connection, and **Remove** ends it. **Off until the administrator sets `BRIDGE_ENABLE=true`.** New settings `BRIDGE_ENABLE`, `BRIDGE_ALLOW_PRIVATE` (private addresses and `http://`, for two servers behind a VPN) and `BRIDGE_RETRY_MINUTES`. Adds two tables to the data file. The receiver's page is in all seven languages; the owner's side is English in the others until it is translated. See [Using it](docs/using.md#receiving-on-another-immich-server), [Security](docs/security.md#bridges-a-receiver-on-another-server) and the [design](docs/bridges-design.md). (#77, #81 to #88)

### Changes
- **The footer of the pages says which version is running** (for example "Immich Handoff 1.2.0"), taken from the running program, to people who are signed in.

### Fixes
- **A photo tagged while the short tidy-up timer was running could wait until the next trigger.** The tidy-up (`CLEANUP_INTERVAL_MINUTES`) holds the same lock as a pass, and an event that arrived meanwhile left a note that only a pass read, so the event was lost. The tidy-up now starts that pass itself when it ends.
- **Linking an album now hands over what is already in it at once.** Until now a newly linked album waited for the next timer round (15 minutes by default) or for a photo to be tagged, which is what started a pass. Photos added to a linked album later still wait for the timer, as Immich sends no event for an album.
- The administrator is now told (through `NOTIFY_URL`) when a stored key stops working, as the log already said it would; the notification was never connected.

## 1.2.0

### Handing over
- **Hand-overs that expire, and taking the last hand-over back.** An owner can make the copies that arrive **expire**: after N days an unfiled copy (in no album, still carrying the inbox tag) goes to the owner's **trash**, never deleted for good; filing it in an album or taking the inbox tag off keeps it. A sender who joined from the page can **Take back the last hand-over** for 24 hours: the receiver's copies go to the receiver's trash, the originals come back from the sender's trash without the service tag, a copy the receiver already filed in an album stays, and a stack or a live photo is taken back whole. Both need the receiver's key to be able to move photos to the trash, so there is a new setting: `RECEIVER_DELETE`, **on by default**. See [Hand-overs that expire](docs/using.md#hand-overs-that-expire), [Taking back the last hand-over](docs/using.md#taking-back-the-last-hand-over) and [Security](docs/security.md#what-a-key-can-do). Adds a column and a table to the data file. (#53, #57)
  - **Warning, read before upgrading: with the default `RECEIVER_DELETE=on`, the receiver keys of the services made by earlier versions are given `asset.delete` (move to the trash) the next time their owner opens the page after the app restarts.** The app cannot do it at boot: only the owner's own login can change a key, so it happens when each owner next opens the page (which is also when they set a lifetime). The app never deletes for good, and it moves to the trash only copies it handed over itself that were never filed in an album. To keep keys as they are, set `RECEIVER_DELETE=off` in `.env` **before** restarting: new keys are then made without the permission, and a key that already has it **loses it** the next time its owner opens the page after a restart with the setting off (the same way round). Keys you made by hand in `.env` or the routes file are never changed by the app; add `asset.delete` to them yourself if you want these two features. `immich-handoff doctor` warns about a key that does not match the setting.
- **Quiet hours, rules about what a service accepts, and photos tagged for a date.** An owner can say a service hands photos over only between two times (`01:00` to `06:00`), optionally only for files above a size, so a slow line is used at night; the administrator can set the same for the whole app (`QUIET_HOURS`, `QUIET_LARGE_MB`, and `TZ` for the time zone). An owner can also say what a service takes in: a minimum rating, photos only, landscape or portrait, a largest file. A photo that does not match is not handed over and gets the refused tag, and the activity says so; this makes a service a feed for a photo frame. A sender can tag a photo for a date (`share/2027-06-01`) and it is handed over on that day. All three are on the owner's service settings, the routes file and, for a plain install, the environment, and are in every language. Adds seven columns to the data file. See [Quiet hours and what a service accepts](docs/using.md#quiet-hours-and-what-a-service-accepts) and [Tagging a photo for a date](docs/using.md#tagging-a-photo-for-a-date). (#55, #50, #52)
- **A stack is handed over whole.** Tagging any photo of a stack (a burst, or a RAW and JPEG pair) hands over every photo in it, the cover first, and makes the stack again at the receiver's, with the same cover. With **move** the whole stack goes to the sender's trash; each photo counts against a daily limit. This needs `stack.read` on the sender's key and `stack.create` on the receiver's: new keys have them, and a key made earlier is given them when its owner next opens the page. Until then, or for a key made by hand without them, only the tagged photo goes (or the photos arrive separately) and the setup check warns. (#25)
- **A live photo is handed over with its motion.** The motion clip (a separate hidden video in Immich) goes too, hidden, and the still is linked to it, so the receiver's copy plays. With **move**, the still and its clip go to the sender's trash together. If the clip cannot be read only the still is handed over, as before. (#24)
- **Details that Immich keeps only in its database now arrive.** An **archived** photo stays archived at the receiver, and the **description, rating and location** a person added in Immich travel as an XMP sidecar the receiver's Immich reads (the photo file and the checksum check are unchanged). Edits (rotate, crop) still do not travel. On by default; `CARRY_DETAILS=off` turns it off for the whole app. It needs no new permission. A description arrives as the receiver's own, so a sender who keeps private notes there should know. See [what is carried over](docs/what-is-carried-over.md) and [settings](docs/settings.md). (#23)
- **Hand over a whole album.** A sender can link one of their own albums to a service they joined from the page. Everything in it, now and added later, is handed over once, with no tag; the key gets `album.read` through the person's own login. Albums are checked on a timer (`ALBUM_CHECK_MINUTES`, default 15) because Immich has no album event. A big album hand-over adds a line to Recent events and notifies `NOTIFY_URL`. Adds two columns to the data file.
- **Arrivals can go into an album.** A service can name an album (**More options** when creating it on the page, `album:` in a routes file, `ALBUM` for a plain install). Handed-over photos go into that album of the receiver's account, made when the first photo arrives, instead of being tagged inbox; if it cannot be used the photo is tagged inbox as before and the problem is reported once. Such a key also gets `album.read`, `album.create` and `albumAsset.create`. Adds a column to the data file, so going back to an older version afterwards is not supported.
- **Pause and resume.** A sender can pause their own sending to a service, and an owner a whole service: nothing is handed over, photos tagged meanwhile stay tagged, and on resume they go at once. Each side sees the other's pause. See [Using it](docs/using.md#pausing). (#7)
- **Daily limits per sender.** An owner can cap what each sender hands over in a day (photos, megabytes, or both). Photos over the cap wait, still tagged, and go by themselves when the oldest hand-over is 24 hours old or the cap is raised. Senders see their use and the wait, owners see each sender's use, and `NOTIFY_URL` is told once. Adds columns and a small table to the data file. See [Using it](docs/using.md#daily-limits-for-senders). (#12)

### The page
- **A new look.** The page is redesigned: the twilight palette of the app icon in a light and a dark theme (a button in the bar switches; the choice is remembered in the browser), glass-style cards, fonts the app serves itself, and three views chosen from tabs (**Overview**, **Services**, **Activity**). The Overview opens with a **Needs you** strip (requests, lost or removed connections, paused services, reached limits) and **This week**, a chart of the last seven days with what waits and what is in your inbox; new people see a **Get started** guide. Services are rows that open in place, grouped into **You send to**, **You receive**, **You offer** and **You can join**, and every question is asked in a dialog in the page's style. The bar has **Search** (a command palette on **Ctrl K** / **Cmd K**) and an arrival chime. The light behind the page drifts, cards tilt toward a mouse and numbers roll, all skipped for reduced motion and touch screens, and nothing runs while idle. The sign-in, expired-invitation and invitation pages, the offer form and the administrator's page follow the same look. Nothing a service can do changed. (#3)
- **A live scene on the Overview.** Each service you take part in has a stage with a lane for every sender and a gate into the receiving library. For every real hand-over a small square travels along the lane and an **arrived while you watch** counter ticks; a paused lane, a daily limit (with when it ends) and a lost connection are shown with words as well as colour. It shows no photo or file name, does nothing while idle and respects reduced motion. It is built on a live stream of typed events (a photo started, handed over, refused or failed, held by a limit, a pause, a lost or restored connection), each sent only to the people it concerns and to an Immich administrator, a burst being one event with a count. See [Using it](docs/using.md#the-live-scene) and [How it works inside](docs/architecture.md#live-events).
- **The pages in seven languages.** English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese (Brazilian wording) and Russian. The page follows your browser's language, and the **language menu** in the bar (the two-letter code) picks one for good (a cookie in this browser). Only what you read on the pages is translated: the log, the setup check, the documentation and the `NOTIFY_URL` messages stay English. Events already in the activity list are shown in your language too. The translations are first drafts and a native speaker's corrections are welcome (see [How it works inside](docs/architecture.md#languages)). (#16)
- **Install the page on a phone.** The page has a web app manifest, icons in the sizes phones ask for and theme colours, so it can be added to the home screen: **Install app** in Chrome on Android, **Share, Add to Home Screen** in Safari on iPhone. It needs HTTPS. There is no service worker and nothing is cached, so no signed-in page is ever kept. See [Operations](docs/operations.md#installing-the-page-on-a-phone).
- **A way into the page from inside Immich.** The app serves `static/immich-button.js`; one line added by your reverse proxy to Immich's pages loads it, and it puts the Handoff icon in Immich's own header, between the notification bell and the person's avatar, so nobody has to type the page's address. It falls back to a round button at the bottom right if Immich's header changes. [Make Handoff easy to find](docs/find-handoff.md) has the tested nginx configuration, the login-page message tip and what does not work. A browser test checks the placement against the pinned Immich. (#44)
- **Share the page and invitations more easily.** Next to an invitation link are **Copy a message** (a short ready-written invitation in the page's language) and **QR code** (drawn by the app, black on white so a phone can scan it from the screen); the administrator's page has a **Share this page** box with the same. Adds one dependency, `rsc.io/qr`. (#44)
- **The page notices when Tags are off** in a person's Immich account (Immich has them off by default) and offers a **Turn on Tags** button, which changes that one setting with their own session. They still need to refresh Immich afterwards.

### Running it
- **A setup check.** `docker compose exec immich-handoff /immich-handoff doctor` tests the setup and says how to fix what fails: the secrets, `PUBLIC_WEBHOOK_URL`, that Immich is reachable and new enough, that each stored key works and has the permissions it needs, that Tags are on, that each sender's workflow exists and matches, how Immich's last workflow runs ended, and the backup settings. It only reads, and exits 1 when something failed (warnings do not). Keys made from now on also get the read-only `workflow.logs`; older keys work and the check says how to add it. See [Troubleshooting](docs/troubleshooting.md#check-your-setup-first). (#13)
- **An administrator's page.** An Immich administrator gets an **Admin** link (everyone else gets "not found") listing every service with its owner, members and the health of each connection, recent events across all services, **Check now** (the setup check on demand), and the power to **remove a service or a member**. Removal makes the app forget the key and stop handing over, tells the people involved and `NOTIFY_URL`; it cannot delete anyone's API key (only its owner can), so the person is told to delete it (**Clean up** does). See [The administrator's page](docs/admin.md). (#14)
- **Backup and restore.** `immich-handoff backup` asks the running app for a backup of its data file; the app makes it at the next quiet moment between hand-overs (after 4 hours it is made anyway, named `-forced`, with a warning). `backup --wait` follows it with an exit status a script can read (0 made, 2 made but forced, 1 failed), `backup --list` shows the files, and with the app stopped it copies at once. `immich-handoff restore <file>` checks the file, refuses while the app runs and refuses to overwrite data without `--force`. The app now holds a lock on its data folder, so a second copy on the same data stops with a clear message. See [Operations](docs/operations.md#backups), which also has a walkthrough for moving to another machine. (#15)
- **The app makes backups on its own.** By default every 24 hours (`BACKUP_EVERY`, or `off`), waiting for a quiet moment, keeping the newest 7 (`BACKUP_KEEP`); only these `handoff-auto-…` files are ever removed. `BACKUP_DIR` can point at a second mounted folder. A failure is logged, written to the recent events, sent to `NOTIFY_URL` and tried again an hour later; the administrator's page shows the schedule, the newest backup and the last failure. See [Automatic backups](docs/operations.md#automatic-backups). (#29)
- **Download a backup from the administrator's page, if you switch it on.** `HANDOFF_ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD=true` adds a **Download backup** button. It is off by default because the file holds every stored key (encrypted) and everyone's name and email. When on: administrators only, a confirmation, one download every 10 minutes for the whole app, each one written to the recent events and sent to `NOTIFY_URL`, the copy removed as soon as it is sent. See [Download a backup](docs/operations.md#download-a-backup-from-the-administrators-page) and [Security](docs/security.md#backups). (#28)
- **Nightly images.** Every night at 09:00 UTC, and only if `main` changed, an image is built from `main`, tested like a release, signed, and published to Docker Hub and GitHub's registry as `20261107-nightly` (the date) and `nightly` (always the newest), so `image: vfuoglio/immich-handoff:nightly` follows `main`. The newest 14 dated tags are kept; `latest` and release tags are never touched. See [Releasing](docs/releasing.md#nightly-images) and [Operations](docs/operations.md#upgrading). The workflow has not run on GitHub yet.
- **Release hygiene.** Every release image is **signed** (cosign, keyless) and carries a **software bill of materials** and build provenance, and the workflow checks both. The GitHub Actions are current and **pinned by commit hash**; Dependabot proposes weekly updates into `develop`. A fix for an older version (1.1.1 after 1.2.0) no longer takes the `latest` tag. (#9)
- **Container health check and Docker Hub.** The image reports `healthy` or `unhealthy` in `docker ps` (a built-in `immich-handoff --healthcheck`; the image has no shell or curl), and is also published on Docker Hub as `vfuoglio/immich-handoff` besides `ghcr.io/vfuoglio/immich-handoff`.

### Tests and documentation
- **The end-to-end tests run in three groups at the same time.** They used to run one after another, about five minutes, and CI waited for the unit tests first. Now `test/groups.sh` splits them into three groups of about the same length (`browser`, `core`, `hub`), CI runs the three together on separate machines (each with its own throwaway Immich) and does not wait for the unit tests, so a pull request waits for the longest group, not for the sum. The check that branch protection asks for, `e2e`, is kept, green only when every group is; a script fails when a test is in no group or in two. `test/run.sh browser` (or `core`, `hub`) runs one group, and `test/run-parallel.sh` runs the three on one machine (only faster with CPU to spare). A race in the carry-over test (a hand-over could carry the date from before an edit that Immich had not applied yet) showed up under the load and is fixed. (#58)
- **The page is tested in a real browser.** The end-to-end run also opens the real page in headless Chrome, including a phone-sized window, and checks what a person sees and does (see [How it works inside](docs/architecture.md#testing)). It found and fixed three faults: the numbers on the Overview refreshed in a loop for as long as the page was open, the rolling digits never rolled after the first paint, and an open service row closed itself after every action. A test also checks what is carried over against a real Immich (`test/e2e/carryover_test.go`).
- **The pictures in the README and the documentation are remade** for the new design (all in English), and on a phone the bar's buttons no longer spill off the right edge. The README moves: an animation of the live scene near the top and a **In action** table of six more (the numbers rolling during a hand-over on a desktop and a phone, the command palette, the theme switch, a service opening in place, an invitation with its QR code), all recorded from the real page in the light and the dark theme; [how they are made](tools/screenshots/README.md#the-animations).
